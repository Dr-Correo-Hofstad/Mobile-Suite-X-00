When you mechanically compress or strike a specialized element in your robot's shock absorbers, it generates an electrical pulse, which your network then routes to the capacitors and the field generator.

However, it is important to clarify how a capacitor works compared to the shock absorber element in this sequence:

1.  The Shock Absorber (The Generator): Smacking or compressing the landing gear is what actually *creates* the raw energy pulse. This is typically done using piezoelectric materials (crystals/ceramics that output high voltage when compressed) or a linear electromagnetic generator (a magnet passing through a coil via spring impact).
2.  The Capacitor (The Storage Buffer): The capacitor does not generate energy when smacked; instead, it acts like an electrical sponge. It catches the rapid, chaotic spike of electricity coming from the shock absorber impact, holds it safely for a split second, and then releases a clean, controlled burst of current to power your next step.
3.  The Field Generator (The Destination): By using the asymmetric wiring network we laid out, the sudden surge that overflows past your capacitor's safe limit is funneled directly into the field generator coil. The coil absorbs this sudden heavy rush of current and instantly converts it into an electromagnetic field.

The Flow of Energy During a Landing:
------------------------------------

-   Impact: Your robot's foot strikes the ground → The shock absorber translates that mechanical force into a high-voltage electrical pulse.
-   Absorption & Split: The pulse hits your wiring node → The initial microsecond spike rushes down the thin wire to prime and charge your capacitors to their safe limit.
-   Diverting to the Field: Once the thin wire hits its bottleneck and choked-path resistance, the remaining heavy bulk of the impact current is forced down the thick, low-resistance path → This dumps the massive energy surge into the field generator, safely projecting the field without overloading your storage cells.
