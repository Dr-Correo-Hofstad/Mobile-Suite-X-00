## How the Passive Reflex System Works## 1. Low-Impact Mode (Running & Take-off)
During normal operations, the shock absorbers generate lower-voltage pulses. The High-Voltage Zener Diode on the trauma path acts as a closed gate because the voltage hasn't crossed its threshold. The energy flows naturally down the normal path to fill your primary capacitors and feed your field generator for momentum.
## 2. Trauma Shock Mode (Debris Impact)
When a piece of space debris slams into the armor, the instantaneous voltage spike from the harvest matrix will be massively higher than a normal footstep.

* The Gate Opens: This giant voltage spike instantly blows past the threshold of the trauma path's Zener diode.
* Life Support Surge: The massive energy pulse floods the Heavy Power Bus, which is directly tied to life support. This extra energy drives an instantaneous boost mode—cranking up oxygen scrubbing, pressure stabilization, or temperature controls to counteract potential hull fractures.
* Auto-Open Defenses: The same high-energy pulse is routed directly to the firing or deployment mechanisms of your defense systems (e.g., explosive bolts, spring-loaded deployment rails, or electromagnetic countermeasures). The impact itself provides the raw electrical power needed to physically throw open the defense hatches and activate them, completely bypassing software delays.

------------------------------
## Preventing System Blackouts: Diode Isolation
To ensure that a failure in one system doesn't drain power from the others during a trauma event, every critical system must be isolated using Schottky Blocking Diodes.
If a piece of debris causes a localized short-circuit in the defense systems, the blocking diodes prevent life support power from draining backward into that short. Life support will remain fully powered by the primary capacitor bank, keeping the vital systems online while the robot automates its defense response.
