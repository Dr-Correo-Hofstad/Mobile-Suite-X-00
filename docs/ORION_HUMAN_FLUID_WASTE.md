SYSTEM OVERVIEW & CORE DESIGN
--------------------------------

The Orion Fluid Evacuation System is a 100% mechanical, zero-software life-support peripheral engineered explicitly for deep-space and atmospheric airframes. By completely bypassing binary bottlenecks, sensors, and electrical wiring, the system eliminates the risks of digital software glitches, electrical short circuits, and firmware failures during high-G combat or complete power loss scenarios.

The platform utilizes a dual-stage manual pressure gradient to establish a secure fluid path from the pilot's anatomical collection interface straight into the vacuum of space, completely clearing the vehicle's interior envelope without breaking primary hull seal compliance.

STEP-BY-STEP OPERATIONAL PROTOCOL
------------------------------------

Stage A: Primary Collection & Buffering
---------------------------------------

1.  Secure Connection: Verify the flexible silicone transfer hose from your direct-insertion cup is locked onto the buffer tank's intake nozzle port.
2.  Anatomical Alignment: Carefully place the intra-cup guide tip into position to establish a close anatomical seal.
3.  Generate Suction: Manually compress the wall-mounted rubber bellows pump. This creates an immediate negative pressure gradient within the 1,200 mL buffer reservoir.
4.  Fluid Capture: Proceed with relief; the internal vacuum sweeps all fluid downstream through the one-way Viton flapper valve, isolating it inside the titanium wall tank.

Stage B: Universal Overboard Discharge Sequence
-----------------------------------------------

1.  Bridge the Circuit: Take the outbound discharge line from the buffer tank and snap its coupler securely onto the wall-mounted bulkhead nipple.
2.  Check Gasket Clearance: If routing the line out through a hatch seam, ensure the line tracks cleanly through the designated AC Delco pressure sleeve to preserve the hull's sealing threshold.
3.  Engage the Vacuum: Pull the manual isolation lever down 90 degrees into the vertical *OPEN* position to connect the buffer tank to the vacuum of space.
4.  Evacuate the Reservoir: Compress the inline manual bulb pump or let the pressure difference draw the liquid out through the exterior extension tube.
5.  Lock Down the Airframe: Throw the isolation lever back into the *CLOSED* position and uncouple the hose from the wall nipple, restoring complete environmental compartmentalization to the cabin.
